Your priorities

One swing priority, one aim priority and one short-game priority. Everything else stays as it is.

Swing priority: stay in posture through the ball on driver, 3 wood and hybrid

Across 422 long-club shots, the ones where you hit down through the ball were far cleaner than the ones where the club was travelling level or upward at impact. In your recent sessions, tops happened on 5% of the steeper shots and 27% of the shallower ones. Your good long-club shots are the ones where you stay down and trust the loft. The ugly ones are where you lift or scoop, which fits the early extension you have already identified in your own swing.

  1. Setup. Grip pressure 4 out of 10. Driver ball just inside your lead heel and tee it low, about half the ball above the crown. A high tee invites the lift.
  2. Feel. Keep your chest over the ball until it has gone, and keep pressure through the middle of your feet, not the toes.
  3. Accept a low flight for now. Your driver launches at about 7 degrees and still carries about 189 yards. A lower flight is a fair price for cleaner contact. Flattening the angle can wait until your strikes are consistent.
  4. Check it with SwingSync. Success is attack angle steeper than -2 degrees on at least 7 shots in 10.

Correction to earlier advice. I first suggested shallowing your attack angle on the long clubs. The full 14-session data says the opposite for you, so this plan does not use it.

Aim priority: square the 5 iron and stop the right miss

Your 5 iron finishes right 78% of the time, and the median miss is 12.7 yards right with the face about 2.5 degrees open to the target. Your other irons are far closer. This is a set-up fix before it is a swing fix.

  1. Set the stick. Lay a stick or garden cane along your toes, parallel to the target line (D3).
  2. Aim left. Pick the left edge of the green or fairway with the 5 iron and align to that.
  3. Keep the grip. Interlock, two and a half knuckles visible on the left hand, both Vs pointing at your right shoulder.

Short-game priority: one-club chipping and putting speed

Two changes save the most shots. Chip with one club, the same way every time. Putt for distance first and line second. Speed is what removes three-putts.

Drill library

Nine drills, referenced by number in the schedule. Each has a job, a set-up, a measure and the mistakes to avoid.

Shoulder rule. If your shoulder feels tight or sore, skip D2, D3 and D4 and do D1, D5, D6, D7 and D9 instead. Do not swing through discomfort. If it has not settled after a few days of rest, see a physio before adding range volume.

D1

Posture wall

Home, 4 min, no ball
Job
Teach your body to stay in posture instead of lifting.
Set-up
Stand with your back to a wall or a solid chair back, with your glutes touching it. Hinge from the hips into your golf posture. Hold a club across your chest or grip it normally.
Do this
  1. Make slow half-swings, keeping both glutes in contact with the wall until your hands pass your hip on the way down.
  2. Feel your chest staying forward as your hips turn.
  3. Do 3 sets of 8.
Measure
You can finish without your hips ever leaving the wall early.
Avoid
Sliding your hips forward off the wall, or standing up out of the bend.
D2

Hit down through it

Range, driver, 3 wood, hybrid
Job
Turn the posture feel into contact on your worst clubs.
Set-up
Grip pressure 4 out of 10. Tee the driver low. Half-swings first, then three-quarter. Alternate one ball per club, never more than three of the same in a row.
Do this
  1. Two slow rehearsal swings holding your chest over an imaginary ball.
  2. Hit the shot with the same feel, and stop your finish at three-quarter length.
  3. Check attack angle after every ball.
Measure
Attack angle steeper than -2 degrees on 7 shots in 10, and no more than 4 ugly in 10.
Avoid
Trying to help the ball up. A low flight is fine.
D3

Stick alignment

Range, 5 iron
Job
Stop the 5 iron finishing right.
Set-up
One stick on the ground along your toes, parallel to the target line. If you have a second, place it along the ball-to-target line. A garden cane works.
Do this
  1. Aim the face at the left edge of the target, then set your feet by the stick.
  2. Hit 5 balls, check the median offline number.
  3. Take the stick away for 5 balls and compare.
Measure
Median miss under 8 yards right across 20 balls.
Avoid
Aiming your feet at the target but the face somewhere else.
D4

Impact tape strike check

Range, 10 balls
Job
Find out where you actually strike the face. SwingSync does not record this.
Set-up
Put impact tape or foot powder spray on the face of a 7 iron, PW or a club that has been shanking. Hit 5 shots, then read the marks.
Read it
Marks toward the hosel end usually mean standing too close or reaching in. Marks toward the toe usually mean standing too far away. Marks near the top edge point to lifting, and the posture drills D1 and D2 are the fix.
Measure
Most marks inside the central third.
D5

One-club chipping

Home, 10 to 15 min
Job
Reliable contact and distance from just off the green.
Set-up
Use an 8 iron or PW. Ball slightly back of centre, weight about 70% on your lead foot, putting-style stroke with minimal wrist. Chip foam balls off a doormat or carpet offcut onto a towel. Keep well clear of the building work.
Do this
  1. Put the towel about one third of the way to the target.
  2. Land 10 balls on the towel, then move it and repeat.
  3. Change the distance every 5 balls.
Measure
7 of 10 land on the towel.
D6

Putting speed ladder

Home or practice green, 10 to 15 min
Job
Distance control, which prevents three-putts.
Set-up
Three lanes at 3, 6 and 9 feet on the carpet. On a practice green use 20, 30 and 40 feet.
Do this
  1. Three balls per lane.
  2. Each ball should stop on the line or up to 18 inches past it.
  3. Rotate lanes, and never hit the same distance twice in a row.
Measure
6 of 9 in the zone.
D7

Lag circle and pressure putts

Home or practice green, 10 min
Job
Turn long putts into short ones, then hole the short ones.
Do this
  1. Lag: from 20 to 40 feet, aim to stop inside a 3-foot circle around the hole. 10 balls.
  2. Pressure: hole 8 of 10 from 3 feet. If you miss the target, start the set again.
Measure
5 of 10 lags inside the circle and 8 of 10 short putts.
D8

Good, bad, ugly random game

Range, 40 to 60 balls
Job
Practice like the course. New club and new target on every ball.
Set-up
Pick 3 or 4 clubs. Full routine every ball. Change club and target each ball.
Score
Good is 1 point (inside 15 yards of the line, right distance). Bad is 0. Ugly (shank, top, unplayable) is -1. Beat your last score.
Tee test
Hit 10 with your 4 iron and count the balls inside a 30-yard corridor.
D9

Mobility warm-up

6 to 8 min, before every session
Job
Free up the upper-back and hip rotation that limit your turn.
Do this
  1. Open book, lying on your side: 8 slow reps each side.
  2. 90/90 hip switches: 8 each way.
  3. Cat and camel: 8 slow reps.
Stop if
Anything is sharp or pinches. Mild stretching is fine.

Ten rules for the round

  1. Tee off with the 4 iron on any hole with trouble, and 6 iron on the tightest. It carries 175 yards and keeps you in play.
  2. Lay up to a number. Leave yourself a 9 iron, 7 iron or sand wedge, your three best approach clubs.
  3. Aim at the middle of the green. Ignore the flag.
  4. Aim for your miss. Left edge with the 5 iron. Right of centre with the wedges.
  5. Hit a provisional any time a ball could be lost.
  6. Take the medicine. Chip out sideways after trouble. Double bogey is fine. Triple is the score you are trying to remove.
  7. Lag long putts. Beyond 20 feet, your target is a 3-foot circle, not the hole.
  8. Play the yellow tees.
  9. Keep the 3 iron in the bag until you are hitting it cleanly on the range.
  10. Log it. Putts, fairways, greens, penalties in 18 Birdies after every round.

Pre-shot routine

  1. Decide. Pick the club and aim point from the card above.
  2. Align. Choose a spot a few feet ahead on the line and set the face to it, then your feet.
  3. Commit. One rehearsal swing with the posture feel, then go. Grip pressure 4 out of 10.

Data notes

These numbers come from your 14 SwingSync exports (1,552 shots). A few things to keep in mind when reading them.

  • Good, bad and ugly are my definitions, not SwingSync's. Good: within 15 yards of the target line and not short. Bad: 15 to 30 yards off, or 15 to 40% short. Ugly: a shank, a top, 30+ yards off or 40%+ short.
  • Sample sizes are small for some clubs. The 3 iron (8 shots), gap wedge (4), 7 iron and 8 iron (10 to 11 each in September) are indicative only.
  • The 21 August export has no left or right sign on offline distance, so I inferred direction from launch direction and spin. It only affects that session.
  • SwingSync does not record where the ball hits the face. D4 is how you find out.
  • Attack angle readings on a range mat can differ from grass. Use them to compare your own sessions, not as absolute numbers.
  • The link between attack angle and ugly shots is a strong association, not proof of cause. The drills test it: if D2 does not improve your contact, tell me and we change the plan.
